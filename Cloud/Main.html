Energy at a Glance

Behind every cloud service is a network of physical machines, cooling systems, storage infrastructure, and networks that continuously require electricity.

❄️

Cooling

💾

Storage

Servers and storage systems keep enormous amounts of data available around the clock.

🌐

Networking

Renewable Energy

Powering the cloud with cleaner energy.

As cloud infrastructure continues to grow, renewable energy can help reduce the environmental impact of the electricity required to operate data centers.

☀️

Solar

Solar power can supply electricity to data centers during periods of strong sunlight.

🌬️

Wind

Wind farms can provide large-scale renewable electricity for cloud infrastructure.

💧

Hydroelectric

Hydroelectric power can provide a stable source of low-carbon electricity for data centers.

🔋

Battery Storage

Batteries can store renewable electricity and help balance energy supply when generation changes.

Renewable energy doesn't remove the need for computing. It changes how that computing is powered.

Inside the Data Center

⚡

Power Infrastructure

Grid electricity, renewable energy, backup systems and batteries.

🖥️

Compute Servers

❄️

💾

Storage Systems

Storage infrastructure keeps data available and accessible to cloud applications.

🌐

Networking

The cloud may feel digital, but every request eventually reaches physical hardware consuming physical energy.

Smart Energy Management

Making the cloud smarter.

Cloud infrastructure can use intelligent energy management to balance computing demand, renewable energy availability, performance, and cost.

01

Workload

02

03

04

05

Energy use, cost and performance can be continuously evaluated and improved.

The goal is not simply to use less energy.

Challenges & Future

Building a more sustainable cloud.

Making cloud computing more energy-efficient is not a single problem. It requires improvements across infrastructure, software, energy systems, and computing strategies.

01

Growing Demand

Cloud services, artificial intelligence, streaming, and connected devices continue to increase demand for computing infrastructure.

02

Renewable Variability

Solar and wind generation changes with weather and time, creating challenges for continuously operating data centers.

03

Performance

Energy-saving strategies must still meet application performance requirements and service-level expectations.
